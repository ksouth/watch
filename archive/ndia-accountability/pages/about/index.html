About

About the project - the problem, what it is trying to solve, what it can't solve

The problem

  • There is pressure on the Agency to reduce spending
  • Following the legislation and treating participants fairly results in the Agency increasing spending
  • There is no pressure on the Agency to follow legislation and treat participants fairly - besides public pressure, which the government of the day can manipulate through PR firms
  • The external body, ART, can correct injustice in individual cases. However, it cannot or does not:
    • prevent the Agency committing the same injustice against other individuals,
    • prevent the Agency using tactics to prolong the process,
    • enforce Model Litigant Obligations,
    • compel the Agency to prevent harm occurring to individuals between their illegal decision and the ART decision,
    • compel the Agency to compensate individuals for harm occurring between their illegal decision and the ART decision,
    • prevent the Agency reducing access to external review by favouring non-reviewable decisions over externally reviewable decisions,
    • compel the Agency to follow its own procedures where they sit outside legislation
  • The Agency responds to the pressure to reduce spending and the lack of pressure to follow legislation and treat participants fairly by:
    • Hiding information about procedures (so individuals don’t know what to expect or whether the correct process is being followed)
    • Refusing to provide information about evidence or reports required (resulting in justification for cutting plans or refusing access when these are not provided by the individual)
    • Using deception and outright lying
    • Using communication systems which are difficult to access
    • Requiring evidence they don’t need (so when the individual is unable to provide it, they have justification for cutting plans or refusing access)
    • Prolonging ART cases and contravening Model Litigant Obligations

This project’s vision

  • Providing access to information which is otherwise scattered and difficult to find and make use of
  • A Service Code which spells out Agency staff communication and decision-making obligations regarding participants, their family, nominees, and providers. These obligations are evidenced through the Agency’s actual guidelines and procedures, legislation, Australia’s human rights obligations, and the Disability Royal Commission findings. It will be written by the disability community rather than politicians, so its primary focus remains on our needs.
  • A NDIS Ombudsman, modelled on industry ombudsmen, with power to make binding decisions. Individuals will be able to refer a complaint to the Ombudsman where the Agency contravened the Code and did not provide a suitable remedy in response to the initial complaint.
  • Enough momentum from the disability community and the population at large for a politician to take it to parliament as a Bill, implement it, and give it real power to protect participants.

How this project will mitigate the problems detailed above

  • Making information about procedures easier to find (instead of needing to download thousands of PDFs and read them all to find information, now you can search them all by their text contents).
  • Before implementation: providing information to individuals about what treatment they should be expecting, if the Agency were to follow all its own procedures, Australian human rights obligations, and legislation – so they can tell when something has gone wrong instead of believing it is normal because it came from an authority.
  • After implementation: providing an oversight authority with the power to correct poor treatment of individuals by the Agency

What this project cannot solve

  • Legislation itself: decisions made by the Agency may be legal as well as unfair. This project is aimed at the methods for reaching those decisions and the communication along the way.
  • Redactions or refusals by the Agency regarding FOI documents: it can’t compel information release, only provide access to what is available. Individual requesters need to ask for internal reviews of their FOI request, or an external review by OAIC, if they believe the Agency did not provide all the information they were legally required to.
  • Changing the outcome of an individual case: while it is not implemented, it can only provide information, and after it is implemented, the individual will need to raise their own case to the Ombudsman.
  • Enforcement before implementation: before the Code and Ombudsman model become reality, the project can only provide information about what is reasonable to expect from the Agency. There isn’t any way to make them accountable for not following these expectations. (Hopefully one day, there will be.)
  • Reach: none of this helps people who don’t know it exists, or who find it inaccessible. (If you see a problem with the site’s accessibility, please email me so I can fix it.)
